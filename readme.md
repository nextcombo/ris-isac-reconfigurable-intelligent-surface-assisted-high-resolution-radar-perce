# [RIS+ISAC] Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface-Assisted High-Resolution Radar Perception with Python Code

This paper analyzes the single-baseline perception scenario for user equipment (UE), where UE fails to distinguish multiple targets within a single resolution cell due to interference from multiple targets. The study shows that through reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS), perceptive accuracy can be improved, including detection rate and positioning precision. RIS effectively provides signal diversity and aids in target discrimination. Specifically, assuming there are known clusters of targets, a RIS beam scanning program is used to achieve high-resolution perception. This paper derives the Cramér-Rao Lower Bounds (CRLBs) for channel parameter estimation and perception, as well as the upper bounds for detection probability, theoretically defining and analyzing the concept of coherence. Then, a method combining orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP) channel estimation algorithm with data association is proposed, integrating information from non-RIS signals and RIS signals for perception. Finally, numerical results validate the potential of RIS in enhancing sensor resolution and demonstrate the potential of the proposed method to achieve high-resolution perception with RIS assistance.
Introduction to Integrated Perception and Communication (ISAC) is expected to become an important technological advancement in ultra-5G and 6G communication systems [1]. The motivation lies in the growing demand for key performance indicators in the fields of perception and communication, leading to limited spectrum resources and potential mutual benefits between these two systems [2^,3^]. Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) can be seen as enabling technology for ISAC, which can advantageously control wireless propagation environments [3^,4^].
Perception should not compromise communication performance, hence relying on the minimum requirements for energy, spectrum usage, and latency transmission via pilot signals [1^]. Nevertheless, perception requires accurate and affordable hardware capable of low-cost devices with limited specifications to make it widespread [5^]. It can be observed that either feasible perception performance is determined by limited perception resources or perception performance requirements dictate necessary perception resources [1^].
This paper considers scenarios where RIS is present and designs a complete environmental perception protocol. The RIS can operate in two modes: (i) exclusive mode, where the UE is associated with the RIS and signals can be sent through a dedicated control channel; (ii) shared mode, where the RIS changes configuration based on the published schedule required by the UE. In both cases, the single-base station perception UE attempts to perceive the environment and decide whether to use the RIS in the shared or exclusive mode. The scenario is illustrated in Figure 1, which shows a sensor with limited resolution (i.e., low bandwidth and small antenna array) attempting to discern clusters of SPs, which we define as sets of SPs within the UE resolution unit. Without the RIS, only individual SPs can be detected, which may be crucial for certain perception applications. Considering the vehicle network scenario where people are approaching the car, as mentioned in [6]: if no one is detected, there could be harm. With the support of the RIS, we can improve the sensor's resolution [7], thereby avoiding such failures. The increase in resolution comes from two characteristics: (i) the RIS enhances spatial diversity, allowing observations from different directions of the SP clusters; (ii) the RIS can have high angular resolution [7].
Conclusion In this work, we evaluated the potential of RIS in close-range target perception when line-of-sight links are present. Through theoretical analysis of the perception system (including detection probability and Fisher analysis), we revealed the importance of a new concept of coherence. The conclusion is that if RIS-aided signals decohere faster than non-RIS signals while maintaining sufficient high received signal-to-noise ratio, RIS can enhance perception resolution. The results indicate that when the sensor's antenna array is smaller relative to RIS size and the number of RIS phase configurations is higher, RIS-aided signals can achieve lower coherence compared to non-RIS signals. This improvement in coherence explains the observed enhancement in perception performance (including detection and positioning accuracy) compared to non-RIS signals. Finally, we found that fusion of both signals can lead to an improvement in perception performance when the quality of both signals is similar. Future work will utilize the concept of coherence to guide the optimization of RIS phase configurations, which may further enhance the high-resolution perception capability of RIS-aided signals.
